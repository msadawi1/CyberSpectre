// COMMON QUESTIONS

Everything you wanted to ask before joining.

No experience? No problem. Not in computing? Still welcome. Below are the questions we hear most often — read these first, then reach out if anything's missing.

// 01 — JOINING

Getting in.

/01Do I need any prior experience to join?

No. CyberSpectre is built for beginners. Our training tracks start from zero — Linux basics, networking fundamentals, intro to cryptography. You'll grow into the harder stuff over time.

If you're already advanced, you'll fit right into the elite CTF team and skip the basics.

/02I'm not a Computer Science student. Can I still join?

Yes. CyberSpectre is open to all MMU faculties — Engineering, Business, Creative Multimedia, anyone. Cybersecurity is interdisciplinary, and some of the strongest hackers come from non-CS backgrounds.

/03Is there a fee to join?

It's free. Membership, workshops, and CTF participation are all included. No paid tiers, no subscriptions, no hidden costs. Industry sponsorships cover event logistics and prizes.

/04How do I actually apply?

Three options on the Join page:

1. Fill out the contact form (fastest)
2. Email cyberspectre@mmu.edu.my
3. Drop by our recruitment booth during MMU's club week

We respond within 48 hours.

/05Is there an entry test or interview?

No test for general membership. If you want a spot on the elite competitive team, that's selected from active members based on internal CTF performance — not a separate interview.

// 02 — TIME & COMMITMENT

How much time does it take?

/06What's the typical time commitment?

2–3 hours per week for casual members — that's one workshop session. Active CTF team members spend more, especially during competitions (sometimes full weekends), but that's voluntary.

/07When and where do meetings happen?

Weekly workshops are on Thursdays, 7–9pm, at FCI Lab 4-3 (and streamed to Discord). Industry talks and CTF events are on weekends — check the Events page for the live schedule.

/08What if I can't attend every session?

That's fine. Attendance isn't tracked or required. Workshop materials are posted to Discord afterward. Show up to what interests you.

The only exception: if you're on the competitive team during a CTF, the team needs you to show up.

/09Will this clash with my final-year project / internship?

Honestly — possibly during crunch weeks. We get it. The club is async-friendly and you can step back during exams or busy semesters without losing membership.

Bonus: cybersecurity skills directly support security-related FYPs and internships, so the time often pays back.

// 03 — SETUP & SKILLS

Tools and setup.

/10Do I need a powerful laptop?

No. Any laptop from the last 5 years works. We mostly use cloud labs, browser-based tools, and lightweight Linux VMs.

Recommended: 8GB RAM, 50GB free disk, ability to run a VM (VirtualBox or VMware Player — both free).

/11What software do I need to install?

Nothing before joining. Once you're in, the first workshop walks through the standard setup:

• Kali Linux in a VM (we provide the config)
• Burp Suite Community for web testing
• Wireshark for network analysis
• A Discord account

/12What programming languages should I know?

None required. Helpful but not necessary — pick up Python and Bash as you go. The cybersecurity field is more about thinking than coding for most roles.

If you want a head start: Python > Bash > JavaScript.

// 04 — CAREER & OUTCOMES

Will this help my career?

/13Does the club help with internships and jobs?

That's a core goal. We're building direct pipelines with national cybersecurity companies, multinational tech firms, and government-linked organizations. Active members get first access to internship postings and referrals.

Active members get early access through the Discord #opportunities channel.

/14Will I get any certificates?

You'll receive:

• Participation certificates for completed workshop tracks
• CTF placement certificates for ranked competition finishes
• Letters of recommendation for active members applying for internships

For industry certs (OSCP, CEH, etc.), we don't issue those — but workshops are designed to prepare you to sit them.

/15Can I list this on my resume / LinkedIn?

Absolutely. Active membership, CTF placements, workshops attended, and any committee role are all legitimate resume entries. Recruiters in security actively look for CTF and club involvement.
